# Code of Conduct

ProdKit Storage is intended to be a professional, technically rigorous, and welcoming open-source project. Participation in this repository means agreeing to the standards below.

## Expected behavior

Contributors and maintainers should:

- discuss ideas, code, reviews, and failures with respect and technical specificity;
- critique implementations rather than people;
- assume good intent while still raising correctness, security, reliability, and maintainability concerns clearly;
- make room for different levels of experience and ask for clarification without ridicule;
- disclose relevant conflicts of interest when they could affect a technical or governance decision;
- protect private information, credentials, vulnerability details, and other sensitive material.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following are not acceptable in project spaces:

- harassment, threats, intimidation, stalking, or discriminatory abuse;
- sexualized language or unwanted sexual attention;
- personal attacks, insults, deliberate humiliation, or sustained hostile behavior;
- publishing another person's private information without permission;
- knowingly submitting malicious code, secrets, deceptive test results, or fabricated security claims;
- disrupting discussions or reviews after maintainers have asked for the behavior to stop;
- retaliation against someone who reports a conduct or security concern in good faith.

## Scope

This policy applies to issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, commits, release activity, and other spaces where someone is acting on behalf of this project or the ProdKit organization.

## Reporting

Do not place sensitive conduct reports or personal information in a public issue.

When possible, contact a repository or organization maintainer privately through GitHub. If a private maintainer channel is unavailable, or the concern involves a maintainer, use GitHub's abuse/content-reporting mechanisms.

Security vulnerabilities should follow [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md), not the conduct-reporting process.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit or remove content, close or lock discussions, reject contributions, issue warnings, temporarily restrict participation, or permanently block participation when necessary to protect the project and its contributors.

Enforcement decisions should be proportionate to the behavior, consider repeated patterns and severity, and avoid exposing reporters or affected people unnecessarily. Maintainers who are directly involved in a report should not be the sole decision-maker when another maintainer is available.

Good-faith disagreement about architecture, performance, security, compatibility, or project scope is not a conduct violation by itself. Technical debate is welcome when it remains respectful and evidence-based.
